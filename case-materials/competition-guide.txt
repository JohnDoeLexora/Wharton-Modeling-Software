Investment Competition Guide
Wharton Global High School Investment Competition


Welcome to the Competition
The Wharton Global High School Investment Competition challenges your team to develop, implement, and
evaluate a long-term investment strategy designed to help a client achieve specific financial goals and meet
future cash-flow needs.

Over the 10-week competition, your team will get to know the client, conduct research and analysis,
develop an investment strategy, construct a portfolio, and evaluate your approach.

During the first six weeks, your team will develop and implement your investment strategy using the Wharton
Investment Simulator (WInS). At the end of Week 6 (November 6), you will submit your Investment Policy
Statement (IPS), trading will end, and your portfolio will be frozen.

Along the way, you will demonstrate how your investment decisions reflected your strategy through the Trading
Notes Analysis, formally articulate and finalize your strategy in the IPS, and evaluate how you implemented that
strategy in the Final Report.

Keep the Big Picture in Mind
Your investment strategy is the cornerstone of the competition.

This is not a competition to see which team can make the most money in a few weeks. Your challenge is to
develop a thoughtful long-term investment strategy for your client and demonstrate that your investment
decisions support that strategy.

From the beginning of the competition, your team should develop a strategy and use it to guide your research,
portfolio construction, and investment decisions. The strategy may evolve as you learn more, but your decisions
should reflect a clear and cohesive approach.

Let’s Get Started
Start with Your Client
Before deciding what to invest in, you need to understand who you are investing for and what they are trying
to accomplish.

Begin with the Client Case Study provided in SurveyMonkey Apply and carefully review the client’s financial
goals.

The case study is your starting point. It is up to your team to determine what additional research and analysis

Develop Your Investment Strategy
Once you understand the investment challenge, your team needs to determine how you will approach it.

There is no single “correct” investment strategy for your client.

Your strategy should reflect your team’s research and analysis into how to pursue growth, reliably meet future
commitments, preserve flexibility, and manage risk.

As you develop your strategy, you will need to decide how to construct and diversify the portfolio, how its
components will support different needs, how the portfolio’s asset composition may change over time, and
which principles will guide decisions as funding dates approach and payments are made.

The goal is not simply to identify investments you think will perform well. The goal is to develop a cohesive
strategy in which your investment decisions work together to support the client’s objectives across a range
of possible market outcomes.

The IPS: Where Your Strategy Comes Together
Your Investment Policy Statement (IPS) is where your team formally articulates and finalizes its investment
strategy.

Your research, analysis, Trading Notes, and experience implementing your strategy should all contribute to
the IPS.

Review the complete Investment Policy Statement Requirements (located under the Pages tab => Deliverables
on your team’s SurveyMonkey Apply account) at the beginning of the competition, so you understand what you
are working toward.

Once the IPS submission deadline passes, your investment strategy is final and may not be revised. Your
portfolio and Final Report should reflect the strategy established in your IPS.

Putting Your Strategy into Action
The Role of WInS
WInS provides your team with the opportunity to put your investment strategy into action using real-world
market conditions.

Use the simulator to research investments, construct your portfolio, make investment decisions, and experience
how changing market conditions affect your holdings.



                                                                                    Investment Competition Guide   2
WInS is a tool for implementing your investment strategy. It is not the competition scorecard, and gains
or losses generated during the WInS trading period should not be added to or subtracted from the client’s
long-term portfolio projections. Those projections should begin with the cash flows provided in the
Client Case Study and use reasonable return assumptions consistent with your strategy.

You are not being evaluated based on:
• Your portfolio ranking
• How many trades you make
• Whether you outperform other teams
• Whether your portfolio makes money during the competition

A long-term investment strategy cannot be judged solely by what happens in the market over a few weeks.
Instead, your portfolio provides evidence of the decisions your team made and how you put your strategy
into practice.

Document Your Decision-Making
The decisions you make throughout the competition matter.

When your team executes a trade in WInS, the Trading Note should capture the reasoning behind the decision,
including its alignment with your strategy, the supporting research or analysis, and its expected role in growth,
liquidity, risk management, or future funding.

The Trading Notes Analysis is due at the end of Week 4 (October 23), two weeks before your IPS is due.

For the Trading Notes Analysis, your team will select three Trading Notes and reflect on how those decisions
supported, tested, or refined your strategy. Although your strategy may continue to evolve before the IPS is
submitted, your investment decisions should already reflect a clear strategic approach.

Do not treat Trading Notes as an afterthought. They provide a record of how your team put its strategy
into practice. Review the complete Trading Notes Analysis Requirements (located under the Pages tab =>
Deliverables on your team’s SurveyMonkey Apply account) at the beginning of the competition, so you
understand what you are submitting.

Your Competition Road Map
The competition is designed as a progression. Each phase builds on the work that came before it.

1. UNDERSTAND
Get to know your client and the investment challenge.
Study the Client Case Study, financial goals, future funding commitments, and sources of uncertainty.
Determine what additional research will help you understand the investment problem.

2. DEVELOP
Build your investment strategy.
Use research and analysis to establish the principles that will guide portfolio construction and balance growth,
liquidity, funding reliability, and flexibility.




                                                                                    Investment Competition Guide    3
3. IMPLEMENT AND DOCUMENT
Put your strategy into action.
Use WInS to construct a portfolio that reflects your strategy, document the reasoning behind your trades,
and demonstrate your decision-making through the Trading Notes Analysis.

4. ARTICULATE
Formally present and finalize your strategy in the IPS.
Clearly communicate the investment strategy your team developed, including how it addresses risk and
uncertainty, as well as the framework that guides its investment decisions.

5. EVALUATE
Assess your strategy and its implementation in the Final Report.
Evaluate your implementation and explain why the strategy supports the client’s operating commitment,
facility contribution, financial flexibility, and communication with co-sponsors across varying outcomes.

When the IPS Is Submitted
The IPS marks an important transition in the competition.

Once the IPS submission deadline passes:
   • Your investment strategy is final.
   • Trading ends and your WInS portfolio is frozen.
   • You may not change your holdings.



                                                                                    Investment Competition Guide        4
Your team then moves from implementation to evaluation and reflection.

A long-term strategy may include planned adjustments as funding dates approach, but it should not be
rewritten simply because markets move or hindsight reveals a different outcome.

You may identify decisions you would make differently, but you may not redesign your strategy after observing
the results. Your focus now shifts to evaluating your implementation, reflecting on what you learned, and
explaining why your strategy is appropriate for the client.

What Are We Looking For?
The competition recognizes thoughtful strategy, research and analysis, client understanding, disciplined
decisions, management of risk and uncertainty, communication, and creativity.

Judges want to understand not only what your team decided to do, but also the reasoning, assumptions, and
tradeoffs behind those decisions.

Your work should demonstrate an understanding of the client, informed investment decisions, and clear
communication of how your approach supports the client’s goals while addressing risk and uncertainty.

Review the Evaluation Criteria before you begin (located under the Pages tab => Deliverables on your team’s
SurveyMonkey Apply account) and revisit them throughout the competition.
